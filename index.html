Objection handling

I’m not interested in signing up for anything.

Speaking with a specialist doesn’t obligate you to enroll. The purpose is to explain possible options, costs, and risks so you can decide whether any are appropriate. Would you like that information, or would you prefer to end the review here?

Is this a government program?

No. DFPHV is an independent information and referral service, not a government agency. Some public assistance resources may be available separately, but the debt resolution providers we refer consumers to are independent organizations.

How much does this cost?

Your initial review with DFPHV is free. A participating provider may charge fees for services, depending on the program. The provider must explain its fees, terms, and risks before you decide whether to enroll.

Can you guarantee my debt will be reduced?

No. We cannot guarantee debt reduction, creditor participation, approval, or a specific settlement. The receiving specialist can explain what may be possible and the risks involved.

Will this affect my credit score?

Some debt relief approaches can negatively affect your credit, especially if they involve missed payments or delinquent accounts. Others work differently. The specialist should explain likely credit implications before you decide.

I don’t want to give out personal information.

For this initial review, we only need basic contact and financial information relevant to your request. Please do not provide Social Security numbers, bank passwords, or full account credentials to me. I can explain what would be shared before any transfer.

I need to think about it.

Of course. This is an important financial decision. Is there a particular question I can answer before you decide whether you’d like a consultation? You’re also welcome to review your options independently.

I’ve already tried a debt relief company.

Are you currently enrolled in a program, or was that something you tried previously? Existing agreements or negotiations could affect which options are appropriate.

I don’t have money to make payments.

If there’s currently no room in your budget after essential expenses, a payment-based debt settlement program may not be suitable. We can instead discuss free counseling and hardship resources.

Can you just email me the information?

Yes, we can provide available information about assistance types and participating providers, subject to your communication preferences. You don’t have to agree to a transfer to receive general information.

Why are you transferring me to another company?

DFPHV helps consumers identify potential assistance resources and connect with participating providers. We don’t directly negotiate or settle debts. The receiving company would conduct its own review and explain its services. DFPHV may receive compensation for the referral.

Are you selling my information?

DFPHV may receive compensation for referrals. We will explain what information would be shared and with whom, and ask your permission before the transfer. You can decline.

Debt-specific questions

Credit card and unsecured debt
  • Are most balances with credit card companies, personal lenders, or collection agencies?
  • How much do you pay monthly toward these accounts?
  • Are minimum payments manageable?
Student loans
  • Are the loans federal, private, or both?
  • Are you in repayment, deferment, forbearance, or default?
  • Have you reviewed income-driven repayment or other options?
IRS and tax debt
  • Is the balance with the IRS, a state tax agency, or both?
  • How much is owed and for which tax years?
  • Have you received notices of liens, levies, or collection?
Medical debt
  • Is the balance with a provider or in collections?
  • Have you asked about charity care, financial assistance, or payment plans?
Litigation and garnishment
  • Have you received court documents, a lawsuit notice, or a wage garnishment order?
